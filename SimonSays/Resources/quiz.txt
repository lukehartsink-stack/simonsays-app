Materials | What's the release liner on PPF for? | UV protection | Keeping the adhesive clean before install | Stabilising colour | Adding thickness | B | The liner protects the adhesive until you're ready to position the film. Peel it off only when you're about to lay the film.
Materials | What's slip solution used for? | Cleaning the panel | Cooling the film | Letting the film slide on the adhesive before squeegeeing | Hardening the top coat | C | Slip solution (soapy water) lets you position the film. Squeegee it out and the adhesive bonds.
Materials | A matt PPF achieves its finish through... | A different polymer entirely | A textured top coat that scatters light | Removing the top coat | A wax applied at install | B | The film body is the same TPU. The matt look comes from a micro-textured top coat that diffuses reflections.
Materials | What's the typical minimum application temperature most PPF brands specify? | Below 0°C | 5°C | 20°C | 30°C | C | Around 20°C is the usual floor, with 22–25°C as the sweet spot for application. Below the minimum, the adhesive doesn't tack properly and the film is too stiff to shape.
Application | Before lifting the liner on a complex panel, you should... | Heat the panel to 50°C | Plan the stretch direction | Apply slip solution to the panel | Trim the film to final shape | B | Planning where you'll stretch — and where you won't — happens before any film comes off the roll. Once it's down, you live with your plan.
Application | What decides the squeegee direction on a panel? | Always horizontal | Always vertical | The shape of the panel, the stretch, and where the film is already anchored | Always from the centre outward | C | There is no fixed direction. On a perfectly flat, neutral panel, centre-outward works — but that panel barely exists on a car. You squeegee away from the point where the film is already set, following the stretch and the shape of the panel, so water and air keep a route out.
Application | To handle a sharp body line crease, the best approach is... | Stretch harder across the line | Cut a relief slit straight across it | Set the crease first, then work outward | Skip it and squeegee normally | C | Lock the crease while the surrounding film is still loose. Application | After install, PPF typically reaches handling strength after about... | 1 hour | 24 hours | 1 week | 1 month | B | About 24 hours for the customer to drive away and wash carefully. Full bond strength keeps developing until the moisture left over from the install has fully evaporated out through the film.
Application | The customer's car should be prepared by... | A wash two days before | Full decontamination and dust-free panels immediately before install | A high-gloss polish only | Nothing — film hides everything | B | Anything trapped under the film stays there. Application | Compound curves (like a wing bulge) are best handled by... | Cutting the film into smaller pieces | Pre-stretching evenly in all directions before laying | Using more slip solution | Setting the centre first, working outward | D | Setting the middle of the curve first gives you a stable anchor. Application | A film edge should normally be... | Trimmed flush with the panel edge | Tucked under the panel edge where possible | Left long for the customer to trim | Sealed with a top-coat spray | B | A tucked edge is invisible and far less likely to lift. Application | A relief cut at a body line should be... | Straight across the line, paint-deep | Just deep enough to free the film, never touching paint | Deeper than the film itself | Made before the liner is removed | B | The blade only ever cuts the film, never the paint. Defects | Orange peel in the top coat usually means the film was... | Insufficiently stretched | Over-stretched | Too old | Applied with the wrong slip solution | B | Over-stretching thins the top coat and disturbs its smooth finish. The texture you see is the strained top coat.
Defects | Lifting at a panel edge a week after install suggests... | Normal settling | The edge wasn't tucked or sealed properly | The film brand was wrong | The top coat was too thick | B | Edges fail when they're not bonded all the way to the substrate. Defects | A whitish "stretch mark" line in the film means... | The top coat got scratched | The film body has thinned and stressed | Slip solution dried out under the film | The adhesive got contaminated | B | The film body itself has been pulled past its elastic limit. The white line is the visible record of where it thinned.
Defects | Contamination spotted under the film right after install should be... | Left alone — it'll work its way out | Lifted, cleaned, and re-set carefully | Heated until it dissolves | Polished out through the top coat | B | Anything trapped is trapped forever. Lift the relevant section while the adhesive is still fresh, clean it, and re-set.
Defects | A bubble at the corner of a body line is most commonly caused by... | Wrong adhesive | Tension across the crease that wasn't relieved properly | Cold film | Too much slip solution | B | If the film bridges the corner under tension, it'll pull away. Defects | A film that won't tack down on initial set is most likely... | Too tacky | Carrying too much slip solution between film and panel | Carrying too thin a top coat | The wrong colour | B | The adhesive can't grip through a layer of soapy water. Squeegee firmly to drive the slip out — the film will then tack.
Defects | Yellowing of PPF over time is mostly caused by... | UV exposure on lower-grade films without proper UV stabilisers | Wrong cleaning chemicals | Heat from the engine | Wax left on by the customer | A | Quality PPF has UV stabilisers in the top coat. Older or cheaper films yellow because those stabilisers either aren't there or degrade faster.
